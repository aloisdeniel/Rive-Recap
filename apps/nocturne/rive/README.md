# Nocturne — 2026 recap (Rive)

A ten-scene, Wrapped-style stargazing recap for the fictional app **Nocturne**, authored as RML with the Rive CLI 1.6.0.
All dates and figures are fictional fixture data. The skies are decorative: none of them is a real sky map.

```
apps/nocturne/rive/
  rive.yaml              project (name: nocturne-recap, main artboard "Nocturne Recap")
  rml/00_data.rml        UserRecap view model (+3 fixture instances), converters, fonts, script asset
  rml/10_main.rml        "Nocturne Recap" artboard: stage, chrome, motif, gestures, the sequencing state machine
  rml/20_aperture.rml    "Aperture Motif" reusable component (iris + observatory reticle)
  rml/30_scenes.rml      ten scene components ("Scene 1 · Invitation" … "Scene 10 · Summary")
  clock.luau             dwell timer (auto-advance) + accessible labels of the two context controls
  layout.luau            responsive layout: reads the laid-out size, writes the portrait / landscape layout into UserRecap
  scripts/               sky.luau, iris.luau, orb.luau (GPU drawables), format.luau (locale digit grouping)
  shaders/               sky.wgsl, iris.wgsl, orb.wgsl
  assets/fonts/          Space Grotesk (variable) + IBM Plex Mono, with OFL licence texts
  fixtures/              sample.json, new-user.json, extreme-long-copy.json
  tools/                 generate.py (+ rml.py DSL), interaction_tests.py, capture_all.py, capture_responsive.py, shoot.py,
                         preview.sh
  captures/              review stills and key transition frames (the walkthrough is ../video.webm)
  build/nocturne-recap.riv   runtime export (unsigned, see "Scripts and signing")
```

The `rml/` files are generated by `tools/generate.py`, which keeps the large, regular parts consistent: ten scene state
machines, navigation transitions that respect visibility, and keyframe completion. Either edit the generator and
re-run `python3 tools/generate.py`, or hand-edit the RML and stop regenerating.

## Improvement pass (October 2026)

The first version worked but read as "solid". Its signature transition was a 320 pt motif shrinking while the
scenes cross-faded. The skies, the moon and the background were flat vector, with a star field of about 70 dots and an
ellipse-stack moon. The copy rhythm drifted with the line count. Scene 6 was a generic window box. The replay
arrowhead pointed nowhere, and hidden Share/Replay buttons stayed in the Tab order. This pass keeps the view model,
the events, the ten scenes, the four personas, the fixtures and reduced motion, and raises the craft.

**Critique that drove it (from the first build and the concept art)**

* The aperture never actually closed *over the screen*, so every cut was a cross-fade with a shrinking ring.
* Concept screen 2 has a dense Milky Way inside the iris. Ours had a blurred band and sparse dots.
* Concept screen 9 has a lit, cratered moon with a warm limb. Ours was a stack of flat ellipses with cartoon craters.
* The background was a flat gradient, with no depth or parallax worth the name.
* A one-line headline left a big gap above its support line, and a two-line one crowded it. The persona title had a
  dead band under one-line names.
* Scene 6's window frame and hatching felt generic. Scene 8's lens floated off the corner of the sheet. The finder
  sat on the wordmark on scene 1.
* The replay arrowhead was a right-pointing triangle off the end of its arc. Focus could land on invisible buttons.
  Large counts had no separators.

**What changed**

Nine anodised blades with a pinwheel overlap close over the outgoing scene with an accelerating shutter, settle with a damped overshoot into a glowing pupil that travels to the next hero, then open with the pupil lingering and the blades flying back with follow-through spin. **3D / shader techniques used.** Everything is scripted GPU rendering: a Luau `ScriptedDrawable` renders a WGSL
fragment shader into a `GPUCanvas` and draws the image in the node's space, so timelines, nesting, opacity and
transforms apply as usual. Every look parameter is a script input keyed by the existing timelines (`propertyValue`).

* `shaders/iris.wgsl`: analytic iris. The hole is the intersection of N arc edges (concave like real diaphragm
  blades). Log-spiral sector boundaries from the hole's vertices decide which blade is on top. Each blade is lit as a
  tilted, curved plate (diffuse + specular), with brushed streaks, a trailing-edge bevel highlight, a contact shadow,
  a lit edge face for its thickness, a hairline rim with a slight chromatic split, and a bloom of light through the
  pupil. Used twice: stage mode (full screen) and housing mode (the motif).
* `shaders/sky.wgsl`: hash-grid stars on three depths, fbm Milky Way with dust lanes, and a gentle twinkle. Background
  (opaque) and disk (soft lens edge, vignette, cool rim) modes.
* `shaders/orb.wgsl`: per-pixel spheres. A moon whose sun angle is the phase, a ringed planet (ring-plane
  intersection, front/back occlusion, planet shadow on the rings), and an eclipse.
* Not used: imported meshes and depth-buffered geometry. The per-pixel spheres and the analytic iris give true
  lighting at any size for one draw each, with no vertex data. A 3D armillary was considered and dropped: the scenes
  already carry enough rings.

**Performance discipline.** No `discard`. One fullscreen triangle per canvas, with no per-frame allocation. A canvas
is re-rendered only when one of its inputs changed: the iris only during transitions, and it issues no pass at all
while the hole is wider than the screen. The sky and orbs also re-render for twinkle or rotation, at most 30 times a
second, and only while their scene is current (`live` is bound to `currentScreen`). A drawable inside a hidden scene
or a hidden persona is not drawn at all.

**Reduced motion.** The stage iris never shows (the motif snaps to its pose, as before). Under `reducedMotion`, pause
or hold, the sky's twinkle time and the orbs' rotation stop, so every shader image is static. Scene static states
carry the final keyed values: full exposure, the gibbous moon, a settled camera. No flashing: twinkle is a slow
brightness modulation of at most ±30 %.

**Fallbacks.** If a shader cannot be created, the iris draws a flat annulus with the same animated hole (the
transition still closes and opens), the background shows the vector gradient and star field kept underneath, and
disks and orbs draw flat vector circles. The no-scripts case is covered under "Scripts and signing".

**New compromises.**
* GPU canvases rendered at a fixed density (2–3 px per point). Since the responsive pass they follow the art scale ×
  the device scale reported to the layout script (see "Responsive layout").
* The stage iris covers the scene for about 150 ms per cut, so the outgoing exit and incoming entrance are partly
  behind blades by design. Scene 1's entrance was delayed by 330 ms so the app-opening iris reveals it.
* The persona-anticipation cut is longer (1.4 s), on purpose for the emotional high point.
* Flex-item texts cannot be keyed in `y`, so headline and support stagger in opacity while their column slides as one.
* The counts now depend on a script converter, so the formatting needs a signed build on the web.

**Performance** (Apple M5 Pro, CLI 1.6.0, headless @1x, CPU-side figures, not device GPU time). `.riv` 649,924 B →
881,640 B. `--bench=600` on scene 1: render encode 0.138 → 0.59 ms mean (p95 1.15 ms), advance 0.017 → 0.03 ms.
Under reduced motion it is 0.156 ms. There is no wasm memory growth. Details are under "Measurements".

**Checks.** `--verify` and `inspect --summary` are clean. `tools/interaction_tests.py` has **20/20** passing:

* `finder_drag_does_not_navigate` now starts its drag at the finder's new position (135, 242). This is an intentional
  change: the finder was moved off the wordmark.
* Two cases were added: `tab_order_only_visible_controls` and `summary_slots_are_share_and_replay`.

## Responsive layout

The recap now fills any canvas. There are two layouts, chosen by one width rule, and each one adapts continuously to
every size on its side of the threshold. It is not one fixed artboard scaled with letterboxing.

**The rule.** The file uses the **landscape** layout when the laid-out artboard is **at least `layoutBreakpoint` wide**
(default **768**), and the **portrait** layout otherwise. Only the width counts: 768 × 1024 and 820 × 1180 are landscape,
and 667 × 375 and 740 × 360 are portrait. `layoutBreakpoint` is a new host-owned `UserRecap` number. The file
reports what it decided in `isLandscape` (and `isSideBySide`, `layoutWidth`, `layoutHeight`). It re-evaluates the rule
on every resize, including in the middle of a scene or a transition. The scene, its state and the progress are kept;
only the layout changes.

| | Portrait (< breakpoint) | Landscape (≥ breakpoint) |
|---|---|---|
| Composition | The hero art is stacked over the copy, as authored at 390 × 844. When the canvas is too short to stack readable copy under the art (phones held sideways: wider than 1.1 × its height, or art below half scale), the art moves beside the copy | Art and copy always sit **side by side** in a centred composition with a capped width (ultrawide canvases keep their margins). The copy column is aligned toward the art: left-aligned on art-left scenes, right-aligned on **scenes 6 and 7, which are mirrored** (art on the right). On tall canvases (768 × 1024, 820 × 1180) the two columns are **staggered on a diagonal**: large art at the top on its side, the copy below it on the other side |
| Copy | Each scene's authored alignment (centre, left or right) | Centre-aligned copy (scenes 1, 2, 4, 9) turns left-aligned, and its divider takes the left-hand form |
| Persona (9) | Emblem over a centred title block | A title page: the emblem in the art column, a left-aligned label, title, divider and description beside it |
| Summary (10) | The tall share card | A **wide two-panel share card**: the seal, wordmark, year, name and persona on the left panel; the stat grid (3 + 2) and headline on the right. The same card is used side by side in portrait. Tall landscape canvases keep the tall card, which fills them better (`layCardT`) |
| Transport | A centred bottom row | A right-hand **rail** (Previous, Continue/Share, Pause/Replay) on wide canvases (aspect ≥ 1.2), a bottom row on tall ones. Portrait side by side also uses the rail |
| Chrome scale | 1 to 1.25 with the size | 1 to 1.5 with the size (1280 × 720 is the reference) |

**What follows the real size.**

* **Backgrounds** bleed edge to edge: the GPU sky and the vector gradient are sized to `layoutWidth` × `layoutHeight`;
  the vector fallback stars scale to cover. Copy and controls keep safe margins (portrait 16–40 pt, landscape 24–72 pt).
* **Text** never scales below its authored size in the chrome (header, labels: the chrome scale is never below 1). The
  copy frame never goes below 0.86 (support line ≥ 10.75 pt, unit label ≥ 12 pt, period label ≥ 9 pt). On short
  canvases the **hero number's box shrinks** first (116 → 84 pt, the font fits the box), and the copy below it moves up.
  The share card's micro labels were raised to 10.5–11 pt, so they stay ≥ 9 pt at the smallest card scale. Decorative
  instrument annotations (bearings, `ALT 60°`, coordinates, the sketch caption; 9–10 pt in design) fade out before the art
  scale would draw them below 8 pt.
* **Controls** keep their 76 × 64 pt hit areas (44–50 pt rings) at every size.
* **No overlaps**: art, copy and controls each get their own region. The header steps aside on the summary only when
  the share card needs the room (the card has its own wordmark and year).
* **Tap zones** cover the real canvas below the header (minus the rail): the left third goes back, the right two-thirds
  go forward, and hold-to-pause works anywhere in them. **Progress segments** span the real width, margin to margin.
* **GPU canvases** are sized to their real box at device resolution. The stage iris and the sky are allocated at the
  canvas size × device scale (supersampled up to 2× on small canvases), and every art drawable at art scale × device
  scale. All of them re-allocate (`GPUCanvas:resize`) when the size or density changes, capped at 4096 px. This also
  fixes the old "fixed 2–3 px per point" compromise.
* **The stage iris covers the whole viewport** at every size. Its centre and hole are keyed in design space and mapped
  through the hero frame; the keyed "open" radius is stretched to the real farthest corner, so the blades start and end
  exactly off-screen and close with no gap at the edges.

**Mechanism.** `layout.luau` is a `ScriptedLayout` in an absolutely positioned box that fills the main artboard, so its
`resize(size, scale)` receives the laid-out size and the device scale. It computes the whole layout and writes it into
`UserRecap` (the `lay*` numbers below); the markup is bound to those numbers. Nothing is laid out by hand twice:

* Every scene keeps its 390 × 844 design. Its hero art sits in an **art frame** and its copy in a **copy frame**: a
  translate and a uniform scale, bound to `layArtX/Y/S` (or the mirrored `layArtXB`) and `layCopyX/Y/S` (or
  `layCopyXB`). The summary sits in a **card frame**. A frame maps a design box (art 0..390 × 100..480, copy 28..362 ×
  458..752, the tall or wide card) into the region the layout gives it. Keyed animation inside the frames is untouched.
* The two arrangements of one element are bound to a 0/1 switch through `a + switch × b` formula converters: positions,
  rectangle sizes, gradient ends, text alignment and text wrap. `laySideT` (0 stacked, 1 side by side) drives the copy
  alignment and dividers; `layCardT` (0 tall, 1 wide) drives the share card.
* The travelling motif, the lodestar and the scene-1 finder live in a **hero frame** (`layHero*`). It equals the current
  scene's frame, and the script **eases it on a scene change (0.1–0.46 s, while the stage iris is shut)**, so the pupil
  travels across the screen to an art column on the other side. It **snaps on a resize**, so a resize settles in one
  frame. The stage iris reads the same hero frame.
* Header, progress row, transport and tap zones are bound to `layHdr*`, `layProg*`, `layPrev*/layPrim*/laySec*`,
  `layTap*`, and are scaled by `layChrome`.

**Host setup.** Use the runtime's **layout fit**, so the artboard is resized to the canvas and re-laid out on every
resize (the CLI's default `--fit=layout` does the same). Check the exact option names in the Rive runtime docs ("Layout"
/ "Fit"). At the time of writing they were: web `layout: new Layout({ fit: Fit.Layout })` plus
`rive.resizeDrawingSurfaceToCanvas()` on resize; Apple `fit: .layout`; Android `Fit.LAYOUT` (`app:riveFit="LAYOUT"`);
Flutter `Fit.layout`; React Native `Fit.Layout`. Give the view the full screen, including behind the system bars if you
want the sky to bleed there, and keep the system insets out of the controls. The rest is in the file. Do not use
`contain`/`cover`: they scale the 390 × 844 authoring size instead of laying the file out. The layout runs in a script,
like the dwell clock, so it needs a signed build on the web (see "Scripts and signing"). Without scripts the file stays
at its 390 × 844 defaults.

**Contract addition.**

| Property | Type | Owner | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| `layoutBreakpoint` | Number | host | Landscape when the artboard width ≥ this (default 768). Non-finite values fall back to 768 |
| `isLandscape`, `isSideBySide` | Boolean | file (layout script) | The layout in use, and whether art and copy sit side by side |
| `layoutWidth`, `layoutHeight`, `layScale` | Number | file | The laid-out size in points and the device scale |
| `lay*` (42 numbers: frames, chrome, transport, tap zones, densities, `laySideT`, `layCardT`, `layNumBox`, `layAnnot`, `layHdrAlpha`) | Number | file | Internal layout outputs. Host: do not write. Their defaults are the exact 390 × 844 values |

**Size limits tested** (`captures/responsive/`, one contact sheet per subject across the whole matrix):

* Landscape: 768 × 1024, 820 × 1180, 1024 × 768, 1280 × 720, 1440 × 900, 1920 × 1080, 2560 × 1080, 1366 × 600.
* Every size covers scenes 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 9 and 10 settled, plus two frames of the 5 → 6 iris (shut and opening).
  Also: the four personas, reduced motion, the new-user and extreme fixtures, and a resize across the threshold in
  the middle of scene 3, in both directions.
* At 390 × 844 the layout reproduces the authored design exactly (`portrait_defaults_reproduce_design`).

**Checks.** `tools/interaction_tests.py` has **26/26** passing: the 20 originals at 390 × 844, unchanged, and 6 new
ones:

* `orientation_follows_width_only`: 767 / 768 / 667 × 375 / 1366 × 600.
* `breakpoint_is_host_tunable`.
* `portrait_defaults_reproduce_design`.
* `landscape_keyboard_taps_and_rail`: Right ×3, the tap zones, the rail buttons and hold, at 1280 × 720.
* `tap_zones_span_real_width`.
* `resize_across_threshold_mid_scene`: landscape → portrait on scene 3, the same in the middle of the 3 → 4 iris, and

The CLI cannot resize a headless run, so that check uses a probe build. `NOCTURNE_RESIZE_PROBE="<s>:<W>x<H>[:<W0>x<H0>]"
python3 tools/generate.py` (run on a temporary copy by the test) sets three script inputs that make the layout script
re-lay out as if the canvas changed at that time. Production builds leave them off (`simAt = -1`).

**Compromises.**

* Portrait on a short, wide canvas (a phone held sideways) borrows the side-by-side arrangement and the rail. Stacked,
  the copy alone would not fit at a readable size under the art at 375 pt.
* One art frame is shared by all scenes, so every scene's art is scaled as one 390 × 380 design box. Scenes whose art
  is off-centre in that box (3, 6) sit a little off-centre in their column. Faint decorative great circles (≤ 13 %
  alpha) can extend behind the copy, as they already did at 390 × 844.
* Tall landscape canvases keep the side-by-side (staggered) layout as the width rule requires, so the art is
  width-limited (≈ 1.2× at 768 × 1024) and the canvas keeps generous sky above and below.
* A resize during the 0.36 s hero-frame glide of a scene change cuts the glide short: the motif snaps to the new
  frame. The stage iris is shut at that moment, so it is hidden.
* Bound text widths do not re-centre a single-line ellipsised text, so the share card's name and persona lines keep one
  width (228 pt) in both cards. The name wraps inside a one-line box instead of a no-wrap ellipsis, so a long name stays
  centred and is cut with an ellipsis.
* The wide card's stat cells are 104 pt wide (inside the stats panel, clear of the card edge). A label with one very long
  word (the extreme fixture's German "Beobachtungsstunden") breaks inside the word and ends in an ellipsis on its
  second line; values shrink to fit their cell.
* Headless captures run at @1x, so device-scale sharpness (@2x/@3x) was verified by the arithmetic (canvas = box ×
  scale), not by pixels.

## Architecture

* **One view model instance.** `UserRecap` has exactly one instance, bound to the root artboard. Every scene and the
  motif are *plain* (non-stateful) nested artboards, so they inherit that same instance; no component owns a copy of
  any statistic. The file also contains instances named `Fixture` (the default), `NewUser` and `Extreme`. These are test
  data a host can select by name; production should create one instance and write into it.
  Rive's `viewModelType="global"` was not used: the CLI's `--data` and the usual host binding APIs target the
  artboard's bound instance, and a global cannot be swapped for a fixture. In practice it behaves as the single global
  model the brief asks for.
* **Sequencing state machine `Recap`** (main artboard), with these layers:
  * `Sequence`: states 1–10 plus "10 settled". Entering state *k* writes `currentScreen = k` and fires `screenChanged`.
    `navNext` and `navPrevious` transitions jump to the nearest *eligible* scene in either direction, with every
    combination of `showScreenN` flags generated explicitly. The same transitions write `navDirection` (+1 or −1).
    1.8 s after reaching the summary, and only while `recapFinishedFired` is false, the machine moves to "10 settled",
    fires `recapFinished` and sets the latch. `navReplay` returns to 1 and clears the latch.
  * `Gesture (tap / hold)`: press, then either release within 380 ms (fires `navNext` or `navPrevious` by side) or hold
    (`isHolding = true` until release, with no tap).
  * `Motif`: the travelling aperture **and the stage iris**. Through AnyState it moves to "Through k" whenever
    `currentScreen ≠ motifPose`. Forward, the full-stage GPU iris closes over the outgoing scene to a small glowing
    pupil, the pupil travels to the next hero, and the blades open on the new scale of sky; the motif and the sky camera
    take their new poses behind it. Back is a mirrored iris (blades overlap and spin the other way, quicker shutter).
    Under reduced motion it snaps straight to the pose and the stage iris never shows.
  * `Control Router`: the centre and right control slots fire `primaryPressed` / `secondaryPressed`; this layer turns
    them into `navNext` or the `shareTapped` event, and a pause toggle or `navReplay`, according to `onSummary`.
  * `Ambient` (sky twinkle, lodestar sway): frozen while paused or holding, static under reduced motion.
  * `Finder Invite`, `Boot` (initial keyboard focus on Continue).
  * `Persona Mapping`: the derived binding described under "Persona mapping" below.
  * `Eligibility`: writes `hasStats` (true when any of screens 2–8 is shown).
* **Scene components**, each with its own machine reading the inherited `currentScreen`, `navDirection`,
  `reducedMotion`, `isPaused` and `isHolding`. States: Hidden → Enter forward or Enter back → Idle (loop) ⇄ Frozen
  (paused) → Exit forward or Exit back → Hidden; Static is the reduced-motion state. Every Enter, Hidden and Static
  timeline is auto-completed with the settled value of every property any timeline in that scene touches. That is why
  rapid navigation always settles cleanly, with no orphaned or stacked poses.
* **Dwell clock** (`clock.luau`, a 1×1 `ScriptedLayout`): keeps `primaryA11yLabel` / `secondaryA11yLabel` in step
  with what the two context slots do, and waits for the entrance to settle (1.6 s, or 0.35 s under
  reduced motion), then fills `screenProgress` over `clamp(autoAdvanceSeconds, 3, 60)`. Non-finite values fall back to
  7 s. It fires `navNext` once per scene. It never runs while `isPaused` or `isHolding` is set, never when
  `autoAdvanceEnabled` is false, and never past the summary. Progress is kept across a pause.
* **Progress segments**: a flex row of ten boxes. Box *k* (for 2–9) has `displayValue` bound to `!showScreenk`, so
  hidden scenes drop out of the row and the remaining segments widen. Exactly as many segments render as there are
  eligible scenes. The fill of each box is `scaleX = clamp(currentScreen − k + screenProgress, 0, 1)`.

### Motion score (all scenes)

| Phase | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Orient | 0–400 ms | The stage iris snaps shut over the outgoing scene (accelerating shutter, 250 ms) as it recedes; the pupil settles with a small damped overshoot and glows with the next sky, travelling to the next hero |
| Hero + count | 400–1,200 ms | The iris opens on a new scale of sky (the pupil lingers, then the blades fly back and settle, spinning on with follow-through). The hero illustration builds while the number counts from 0 to the bound value; the count is driven by the scene timeline (`fxCountN` playhead × value), so it is in step with the art |
| Resolve | 1,200–1,800 ms | Unit label, amber rule, headline, support |
| Dwell | host-set | Restrained loops only: twinkle, slow star-trail rotation, meteor pool, orbit drift. All are frozen while paused |
| Handoff | ~930 ms total, ~750 ms visible | Forward: 250 ms close, 150 ms pupil, 530 ms open (the last ~150 ms only clears the corners). Back: a mirrored iris (blades overlap and spin the other way) with a 200 ms shutter, plus the motif opening from a larger scale and copy dropping in from above |

* **Hero sequences:** the app opens shut, breathes, and the iris opens on the Invitation disk (a GPU sky behind the
  wordmark reveal); Nights (the motif's lit blades part on a dense Milky Way that develops like a long exposure as the
  count runs: exposure, band, zoom-out and rotation are keyed in step); Lunar (a lit 3D moon: the sun sweeps round it,
  new → crescent → half → gibbous, with nine session glyphs landing as the terminator passes); Meteors (a bounded pool
  of five meteors flying out of the radiant, each leaving a long-exposure trail, so the fan builds up); Sketches (pencil
  contours, filaments and stipple, then the loupe shows the real nebula the sketch describes); the Persona reveal; and
  the Summary assembly.
* **Continuity objects:**
  1. The aperture: the stage iris carries every cut (its pupil travels from hero to hero), and one nested motif
     instance travels through all ten scenes. It is the disk frame on 1 and 7, the iris on 2, the instrument hub on 3,
     the midnight pupil on 4, the finder on 5, the radiant's observation port on 6, the loupe on 8, the emblem frame
     on 9 and the share-card seal on 10.
  2. The amber **lodestar**: the same object becomes the ring marker, the arc head, the constellation anchor, the
     meteor radiant, the lunar glint, the pen nib, the persona crown and the card spark.
  3. A GPU sky (three parallax star depths and a Milky Way band) whose offset, scale and rotation change with each
     pose: the sky turns a little further with every scene.
* **Persona reveal:** the pupil "breathes" twice before opening. Each persona has its own emblem *and* choreography:
  * 0, nested nebula aperture: rings ripple outward and counter-rotate around a nested Aperture instance, opening on
    a GPU deep-sky field that zooms out from 2.6× and turns.
  * 1, lunar disk: a lit moon tinted with `personaColor` rises with a back-ease while its phase sweeps and phase moons
    orbit in.
  * 2, orbital reticle: the reticle snaps with a damped overshoot, the orbit draws, and a lit ringed planet (tinted
    with `personaColor`, turning) slides along the projected ellipse.
  * 3, meteor arc: a fast arc sweep round a GPU eclipse (a `personaColor` disk with earthshine detail, streamered
    corona and diamond-ring bead) whose corona flares in.
  The pupil breathes twice (7 → 46 → 9 → 34 → 6 pt) before opening on the emblem.

|---|---|---|---|
| 90 s auto run | 10 | 1 | — |

**Host adapter responsibilities**

* Parse `personaColor`.
* Clamp negatives, NaN and ∞ before writing numbers. The file clamps negatives and rounds for display, but cannot
  detect NaN.
* Supply localised and pluralised unit strings and labels. The file groups digits itself from `locale`
  (`scripts/format.luau`: `1,284,567` en, `1.284.567` de/es/it/nl/pt/tr/id, `1 284 567` fr/sv/nb/fi/pl/cs/ru,
  `1'284'567` de-CH, anything else falls back to en), but has no plural rules or compact labels.
* While `captureMode` hides the chrome, the focused control is hidden with it, so keyboard keys do nothing until the
  host resets the flag (a snapshot lasts one frame).
* Map hidden or unknown metrics to `showScreenN = false`. Zero stays a valid number.
* Set `showScreen9 = false` when there is no persona evidence.
* Set `isPaused` while the app is backgrounded or the share sheet is open, and restore the previous value afterwards.
  The file never auto-advances while paused.
* Set `captureMode = true`, wait one frame, snapshot the summary, then reset it.
* Give the runtime keyboard focus, or rely on the file's initial focus on Continue.
* Expose screen-reader announcements for `currentScreen` changes. Buttons carry `SemanticData` role=button.

## Unsupported techniques and substitutions

* **Iris blades:** Rive has no 3D or per-pixel lighting, so the blades are a WGSL fragment shader (`shaders/iris.wgsl`)
  drawn by a script into a GPU canvas, both for the full-stage transition iris and for the motif's own blades. Without
  shader support the script draws a flat vector annulus with the same hole, so the transition still reads.
* **Count-up:** a text run cannot be keyed to a number. Instead a hidden node's `x` is keyed 0 → 1 and bound
  target-to-source into `fxCountN`, and the hero text uses the formula `round(value × fxCountN)` then the grouping converter.
* **Booleans driving layout or opacity:** booleans cannot bind to `displayValue` or opacity, so they pass through a
  converter chain (negate → ToString → ToNumber).
* **Lunar illumination:** a lit sphere (`shaders/orb.wgsl`): the timeline keys the sun's angle, so the terminator sweeps
  across real relief. No-shader fallback: a flat disk.
* **Raster grain:** none. Everything is vector or procedural; no image is shipped.
* **Responsive layout:** a script computes the layout from the laid-out size and the markup is bound to it, because
  the scenes are keyed, hand-composed art that flex layout cannot scale uniformly. See "Responsive layout". Inside each
  frame the copy blocks are still flex columns, so wrapped headlines push the support line instead of overlapping it.

## Scripts and signing

Scripts: `clock.luau` (dwell timer, context-slot labels), `scripts/format.luau` (digit grouping) and the three GPU
drawables `scripts/sky.luau`, `iris.luau`, `orb.luau` with their WGSL shaders. Local builds (`--once`) are
**unsigned**: they play in the CLI previewer and in runtimes built with tooling enabled, but web runtimes and the CDN
reject them. For a web or production build:

* build with `rive . --publish`, which needs `rive login`, or
* (not recommended since this pass) ship unsigned to a runtime that rejects scripts. Auto-advance, the digit-grouping
  converter (so the counts would render empty) and every GPU drawable would be lost. Use a signed `--publish` build.

Where scripts run but the GPU API or shaders are unavailable, each drawable falls back to vector: a flat annulus for the
iris (the transition still closes and opens), the vector gradient and star field for the background, flat disks for the
sky disks and orbs.

## Honest limitations

* **Locale formatting:** digit grouping is in the file (a small separator table keyed by `locale`); plural rules,
  compact labels and full CLDR formatting are the host's job.
* **Event payload:** the `screenChanged` payload timing is unverified (the CLI does not log plain events). Read
  `currentScreen` instead.
* **Focus order:** fixed. There are exactly three focusable controls at all times; the two context slots change face
  and meaning on the summary. Their accessible labels come from the clock script, so a host that cannot run scripts
  keeps the default `primaryA11yLabel` / `secondaryA11yLabel` strings.
* **Summary grid:** when individual stats are hidden on the summary, their slots stay empty instead of re-flowing.
* **Not verified:** RTL was not verified, as no RTL locale is in scope. Haptics and sound are not used.
* **Frame rate:** there is no on-device frame-rate measurement, only the headless CPU bench above.
* **Editor open:** the `.rev` has not been opened in the Rive Editor, because it could not be exported without a login.
